Phase 4: Failed Proofs

Why can't the smartest people alive solve this? It's not stubbornness: it's barriers. This phase breaks down the Relativization Barrier, the Natural Proofs Barrier, and the Algebrization Barrier, plus GCT and diagonalization. Understanding why proofs fail is essential to understanding what a real proof would need to look like.

📄️ Why Haven't We Solved It?

Over fifty years, thousands of researchers, and almost no progress on the main question. It's not lack of effort. It's that mathematicians have proved, three separate times, that whole families of proof techniques cannot work.

📄️ Turing Machines with Oracles

Imagine a computer with a magic black box attached that instantly answers one kind of hard question. Turing invented the idea in 1939. It sounds like a fantasy, but it's the tool that built the first great barrier.

📄️ The Relativization Barrier

In 1975, Baker, Gill and Solovay built two imaginary worlds: one where P equals NP, and one where it doesn't. Any proof that works the same in both worlds can't settle the question. That rules out the most natural attack of all.

📄️ Limits of Relativization

If oracle-blind proofs can't work, a proof has to look inside the machine. In 1990, a technique called arithmetization did exactly that, proved a famous result that cannot relativize, and briefly made people think the first barrier had fallen.

📄️ Circuit Complexity Primer

Forget programs and tapes. A Boolean circuit is computation drawn as a diagram of AND, OR and NOT gates. Most functions need gigantic circuits, yet we can't prove it for a single function we care about.

📄️ The Natural Proofs Barrier

Razborov and Rudich noticed that every circuit lower bound proof of their time had the same simple shape. Then they proved that, if strong cryptography exists, no proof of that shape can ever separate P from NP.

📄️ Cryptography vs. Natural Proofs

Secure encryption needs functions that look random but aren't. If such functions exist, they hide their own simplicity so well that the natural way of proving hardness can't see it. The thing we want to prove protects itself.

📄️ Interactive Proof Systems

What if a proof wasn't a written document, but a conversation? A weak, coin-flipping verifier questions a powerful prover. This simple change led to IP = PSPACE, zero-knowledge proofs and the PCP theorem.

📄️ The Algebrization Barrier

Arithmetization broke through the first barrier. In 2008, Aaronson and Wigderson upgraded relativization to cover it, and showed that the upgraded version still can't settle P vs NP. A second wall, right behind the first.

📄️ Geometric Complexity Theory (GCT)

If combinatorics and simple algebra are blocked, maybe P vs NP needs the deepest mathematics we have. Mulmuley and Sohoni's program tries to separate classes using symmetry, geometry and representation theory, starting with two almost identical formulas.

📄️ The Challenges of GCT

Geometric Complexity Theory hoped the simplest kind of obstruction would be enough. In 2016, three mathematicians proved it can't be. Here's what that setback means, and what's still alive.

📄️ Diagonalization Techniques

Cantor used it to show some infinities are bigger than others. Turing used it to show some problems can't be solved by any computer. It's the oldest trick in complexity theory, and you can watch it work in a table of 0s and 1s.

📄️ Why Diagonalization Fails for P vs NP

Building a problem outside P is easy: diagonalization does it in one line. The hard part is building one that is also inside NP. Here are the two reasons the oldest trick can't finish the job, and the clever things it can still do.

📄️ Common Flaws in Amateur Proofs

Every year, people announce proofs of P vs NP. Almost all of them make one of a handful of mistakes. The most common: assuming you must try every possibility just because the problem looks like it needs it.

📄️ The Burden of Proof

To prove P = NP, you need one algorithm. To prove P ≠ NP, you need to rule out every algorithm that could ever exist. And a strange twist: if P = NP, an algorithm that works is already written down.

📄️ Summary of the Barriers
