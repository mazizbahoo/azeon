Phase 3: Complexity Zoo

P and NP are not the only complexity classes. This phase explores the full 'zoo': coNP, PSPACE, EXP, BPP, and BQP. We also examine quantum computing and its relationship to NP, busting the common myth that quantum computers will simply 'solve NP-Hard problems'.

📄️ The Class coNP

NP is about problems where a yes has a short proof. Flip it, and you get coNP, where a no has a short proof. It sounds like a small twist. It turns out to be its own unsolved problem, sitting right next to P vs NP.

📄️ Tautologies and coNP-Completeness

A tautology is a formula that's true no matter what. Deciding whether a formula is one is the hardest problem in coNP, and it's tied to an old question: does every true statement have a short proof?

📄️ NP-Hard vs. NP-Complete

Two labels I'd been using almost interchangeably. NP-Complete means 'hardest inside NP'. NP-Hard only means 'at least that hard', and some NP-Hard problems can't be solved by any computer, ever.

📄️ The Class PSPACE

Time can't be reused. Memory can. That one difference creates a class of problems that might take forever to solve but never need much room, and it's where two-player games live.

📄️ EXP: Exponential Time

Finally, a separation we can actually prove. The Time Hierarchy Theorem says that more time really does let you solve more problems, and it pins down a class of problems that are guaranteed not to be in P.

📄️ Chess and Go Are in EXP

Blow chess and Go up to an n × n board and deciding who wins with perfect play becomes EXP-Complete, provably outside P. So how does the app on my phone beat me every time?

📄️ Randomized Algorithms (BPP)

What if an algorithm could flip coins, and was allowed to be wrong one time in a trillion? Some problems seem to get much easier. Whether randomness really adds power is open, and most researchers think the answer is no.

📄️ Quantum Computing and BQP

A quantum computer is not a computer that tries every answer at once. It's a computer whose possibilities can cancel each other out. That one idea, interference, is what defines the class BQP.

📄️ Shor's Algorithm

How a quantum computer would factor huge numbers in polynomial time. Most of the algorithm is ordinary arithmetic you can do by hand. The quantum part is exactly one step: finding when a repeating pattern starts over.

📄️ Will Quantum Computers Solve NP?

Probably not. There's a quantum algorithm that speeds up brute-force search, and a theorem that says, in an important sense, it's the best possible. A square root of exponential is still exponential.
